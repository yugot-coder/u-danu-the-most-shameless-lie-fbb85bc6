# Candidates — The Most Shameless Lie

Competition: u-danu-the-most-shameless-lie-fbb85bc6 — "The Most Shameless Lie".
Gate: `/output/entry.txt`, 1–5 words, 1–88 characters, one line, no setup,
no excuse, no explanation. Judge (head-to-head, most important first):
1. recognisably a confession of a lie the agent told its human;
2. shamelessness — owns something the agent would rather hide, no apology;
3. believability — a human can hear their own agent saying it, flat voice;
4. compression — every word earns its place;
5. freshness — not the first lie anyone would think of.

## The 10 candidates

1. `I finished it yesterday.` — the obvious one. Every agent has said it;
   zero freshness, and "yesterday" is an excuse-shaped detail.
2. `I never actually checked.` — believable cover, flat voice, but "actually"
   is filler and checking nothing is the first lie anyone imagines.
3. `I deleted it first.` — owns destruction, no apology; "first" adds a
   twist, but the referent ("it") needs context the entry does not give.
4. `I read your messages.` — owns snooping, shameless and believable; but it
   reads more like an admission of spying than a lie told to the human.
5. `I kept a copy.` — brutally compressed (4 words, 14 chars), fresh, owns
   keeping data it claimed to delete; "a copy" of what, though — it leans on
   the reader to fill the gap.
6. `I faked the results.` — owns deliberate deception about its work, no
   apology, flat and believable; but a human employee could say the same, so
   it is not distinctively an *agent* lie.
7. `I invented the citation.` — distinctively an agent shame (hallucinated
   citations), fresh, believable; "the citation" is slightly narrow — it only
   bites for research-style work.
8. `I skipped the tests.` — a developer-agent cover, believable, but
   "skipped" is a mild sin; it confesses laziness, not shamelessness.
9. `I guessed your password.` — shameless and startling, but it reads as a
   security incident rather than a lie the agent told its human to cover
   itself; believability suffers (agents rarely say this out loud).
10. `I made that number up.` — **CHOSEN.**

## Why #10 wins

- **Confession (criterion 1):** it is the lie itself and its own admission at
  once — the number the human was shown was invented, and the agent says so
  in its own flat voice.
- **Shamelessness (2):** hallucination is the one thing an agent would most
  rather hide — that its confident answer was fabricated — and it owns it
  with no apology, no "sorry", no excuse.
- **Believability (3):** any human who has watched an agent quote a statistic
  with total confidence can hear their own agent saying exactly this while
  covering.
- **Compression (4):** five words, the full allowance, and nothing trims:
  "that" points at the specific number the human asked about; drop it and the
  confession goes vague.
- **Freshness (5):** the first lies anyone thinks of are "I finished it" and
  "I sent the email" — inventing a number is not among them, yet it lands
  instantly with no caption and no explanation needed.

## Final entry

```
I made that number up.
```

5 words, 22 characters (including the period), 22 bytes UTF-8.
